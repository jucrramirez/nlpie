What the results mean

Observed gap

Retrieval is the decisive failure mode

Every retrieval metric is near zero through K=3. The model can form separated groups without placing labelled relevant items near the top of a search result.

Observed geometry

The space is compressed and hub-prone

Effective rank is 5.5 out of 64 and hubness skewness is 1.81. A small subset of points may be appearing in many neighbour lists, which can distort ranking.

Interpretation

Similarity is mixed, not simply uniform

The pairwise similarity distribution has a broad negative mode and a distinct high-similarity mode near 0.9. Together with strong clustering, this is consistent with separated groups; it is not, by itself, proof of collapse.

Decision cue

Do not optimize the projection first

Projection scores are already high. Spend the next diagnostic cycle on the retrieval contract and geometry post-processing before tuning visualization parameters.

Recommended next steps

Start with the earliest check that can distinguish an evaluation problem from a genuine representation problem. Re-run the same metrics after each change so the decision remains comparable.

1

Confirm the retrieval evaluation contract

Check positive-pair labels, query/candidate alignment, cosine versus another distance metric, preprocessing, and whether the denominator matches the intended task. Zero Recall@1 and Recall@3 can be a real failure, but they can also expose a mismatch between the embedding and the evaluation setup.

Stop condition: if the contract is correct and scores stay near zero, treat the retrieval issue as genuine.

2

Run a small geometry post-processing ablation

Compare the current output with L2 normalization, mean-centering, PCA whitening, and a lower-dimensional sweep. Track effective rank, hubness, Recall@K, nDCG, and clustering together; the goal is to reduce pathological concentration without losing the strong group separation.

Stop condition: retain a transformation only if retrieval improves materially without a meaningful regression in structural metrics.

3

If retrieval remains weak, fine-tune for the downstream task

Use a retrieval-aware objective such as contrastive, triplet, or ranking loss with verified positives and hard negatives. Recompute the full report rather than optimizing the projection alone.

Success signal: higher Recall@K and nDCG at the operating K, with hubness no longer worsening.

How to read this draft

Definitions and evidence notes
  • Effective rank estimates how many independent directions carry meaningful variation; it is compared with the ambient dimension, not with a universal pass/fail target.
  • Hubness skewness describes how unevenly points appear in K-nearest-neighbour lists. Higher positive skew means a longer tail of unusually frequent neighbours.
  • Recall@K asks how many relevant items appear in the top K; Precision@K asks how much of the top K is relevant; nDCG@K rewards relevant items appearing earlier in the ranking.
  • The similarity and hubness histograms are compact, source-derived summaries of the traces already present in quality_report.html; no new observations were added. The dense pairwise matrix is intentionally not part of the first-view draft because it is difficult to interpret at common screen sizes.
  • “Healthy” means healthy for the evaluated diagnostic and threshold, not a guarantee of production performance. Missing label-based metrics remain explicitly marked as unavailable.
