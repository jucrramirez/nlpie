What the results mean

Observed gap

Retrieval is the decisive failure mode

Every retrieval metric is near zero through K=3. The model can form separated groups without placing labelled relevant items near the top of a search result.

Observed geometry

The space is compressed and hub-prone

Effective rank is 5.5 out of 64 and hubness skewness is 1.81. A small subset of points may be appearing in many neighbour lists, which can distort ranking.

Interpretation

Similarity is mixed, not simply uniform

The pairwise similarity distribution has a broad negative mode and a distinct high-similarity mode near 0.9. Together with strong clustering, this is consistent with separated groups; it is not, by itself, proof of collapse.

Decision cue

Do not optimize the projection first

Projection scores are already high. Spend the next diagnostic cycle on the retrieval contract and geometry post-processing before tuning visualization parameters.

Recommended next steps

Start with the earliest check that can distinguish an evaluation problem from a genuine representation problem. Re-run the same metrics after each change so the decision remains comparable.

1

Confirm the retrieval evaluation contract

Check positive-pair labels, query/candidate alignment, cosine versus another distance metric, preprocessing, and whether the denominator matches the intended task. Zero Recall@1 and Recall@3 can be a real failure, but they can also expose a mismatch between the embedding and the evaluation setup.

Stop condition: if the contract is correct and scores stay near zero, treat the retrieval issue as genuine.

2

Run a small geometry post-processing ablation

Compare the current output with L2 normalization, mean-centering, PCA whitening, and a lower-dimensional sweep. Track effective rank, hubness, Recall@K, nDCG, and clustering together; the goal is to reduce pathological concentration without losing the strong group separation.

Stop condition: retain a transformation only if retrieval improves materially without a meaningful regression in structural metrics.

3

If retrieval remains weak, fine-tune for the downstream task

Use a retrieval-aware objective such as contrastive, triplet, or ranking loss with verified positives and hard negatives. Recompute the full report rather than optimizing the projection alone.

Success signal: higher Recall@K and nDCG at the operating K, with hubness no longer worsening.

How to read this report

Metric guide in simple words
Intrinsic space

Are vectors pointing in useful directions?

These metrics look at the overall pattern of pairwise similarity.

Mean cosine similarity
On average, how much do two vectors point the same way? Near 0 means they are not all pointing in one shared direction; a high positive value can mean crowding.
Standard deviation (Std)
How varied are the pairwise similarities? A larger spread means the space contains more different relationships.
Minimum / Maximum
The most opposite and most similar pair found. They show the range, not the typical pair.
Geometry

How much of the space is actually being used?

These metrics find redundancy and points that may distort nearest-neighbour search.

Effective rank
Roughly, how many independent directions carry meaningful information? Compare it with the total number of dimensions.
Similarity to mean
How much do vectors point toward the space’s average direction? A high value suggests the whole space is pulled toward one centre.
Hubness skewness
Whether a few points appear as neighbours far more often than others. Higher positive skew means more “popular” points.
Hubness K
How many neighbours were counted for each point when measuring hubness. It is the search-list size, not a quality score.
Clustering

Do groups look separate and compact?

These metrics evaluate group structure. They do not prove that the groups match the application’s meaning.

Silhouette
Are points closer to their own group than to other groups? Higher is better; 1 means very clean separation.
Davies–Bouldin
How much do groups overlap or spread into each other? Lower is better; 0 is the ideal direction.
ARI
Do predicted groups match known labels, adjusted for chance? Higher is better, but it needs ground-truth labels.
NMI
How much information do predicted groups share with known labels? Higher means more agreement; it also needs labels.
Purity
For each group, how dominant is its most common known class? Higher is better, but it can look good when there are many small groups.
Projection

Does a smaller picture preserve nearby points?

These metrics judge whether a 2D or low-dimensional view is faithful to the original space.

Trustworthiness
Does the projection avoid inventing neighbours that were not close originally? Higher is better.
Continuity
Does the projection keep neighbours that really were close originally? Higher is better.
K
How many nearby points are considered when checking the projection. Results can change with K.
Retrieval

Does search put relevant items at the top?

These are downstream task metrics. They are usually the most direct test of whether the embedding works for search.

Recall@K
Of all relevant items, how many appeared in the first K results? Higher is better.
Precision@K
Of the first K results, how many were relevant? Higher is better.
MRR
How high was the first relevant result? A score near 1 means it usually appeared first.
nDCG@K
How well were relevant results ordered within the first K? It rewards finding relevant items early.
Coverage
How much of the total relevant-item space appeared at least once across the top-K lists? Higher coverage means the search reaches more of the relevant space.
K
How many search results are being judged. Always read the score together with K.
